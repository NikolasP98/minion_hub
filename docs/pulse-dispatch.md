# Pulse decisions and dispatch receipts

Pulse decisions now bind the content revision the owner reviewed. An executable approval commits the decision, immutable action snapshot, owner-bound dispatch and background job in one PostgreSQL transaction. The approval route does not send an RPC. An approval receipt means queued work; a scheduled receipt means the gateway accepted a cron job. Neither proves calendar creation, reminder execution or channel delivery.

## Decision and recovery contract

POST `/api/pulse/proposals/[id]` accepts `action` and `expectedRevision`; PATCH accepts typed `args` and `expectedRevision`. A stale or missing executable revision returns 409. Pending edits increment the revision; deciding preserves that content revision. Identical approval returns the original receipt. Decided content and internal digest content cannot be rewritten through direct SQL. Informational kinds cannot become executable by adding a tool name.

The page, notifications popup and notifications page pass the revision from their rendered card. They retain the committed response before projection refresh. A failed refresh or uncertain transport fences decisions until read recovery. Popup reads are triggered by open, actor, organization and view-capability changes. Receipt publication is untracked refresh output; it cannot trigger another open refresh. Actor changes clear private receipt/read intent and fence late responses. The mounted popup case bounds reads and deliberately releases an old owner receipt after a scope switch. Recovery and reconnect only read authoritative feed/count/receipts; they never replay a mutation. The notifications SSR load preserves its existing access-review, organization-kind and Pulse-view gates. Bound-owner rows are filtered from feed/count/detail/recent views even for another profile in the same organization. Legacy unbound informational cards keep organization visibility. `executed_at` without a dispatch receipt is displayed as unverified history.

Feed, count and individual receipt reads also carry request generations within the same owner scope. Decision admission and settlement retire older reads; the shell's combined count request obtains its Pulse publication ticket before fetching. Superseded successes, errors and recovery continuations cannot replace newer state. Retained receipts are capped at 50, and per-id request tokens are discarded when their calls complete. Store tests and the rendered cases hold pre-approval feed/count reads and an older queued receipt, then release them after a known commit and a newer scheduled receipt.

The single active decision separately retains its latest verified receipt/version until completion. Evicting that card from the recent cache cannot discard this evidence or make an older acknowledgement authoritative. Both the action-runtime result and public helper return use the current pinned receipt; completion or an owner-scope change releases the pin. The mounted page regression reads 50 other receipts while a POST is held, then verifies the scheduled receipt and returned outcome survive the older queued response and failed refresh without another write.

GET `/api/pulse/proposals/[id]/receipt` requires Pulse view capability and owner scope. Recent decisions use bounded `(decided_at,id)` keyset pagination with a maximum of 50. Dispatch projections contain safe state/reason/remote ID fields; credential plaintext and raw provider failures never enter them.

The organization-shared business brain corpus excludes every owner-bound Pulse proposal from both content and terminal identity queries, including the indexing owner's own private proposals. Unbound legacy informational rows remain eligible. Dispatch evidence is explicitly excluded from the corpus inventory. A restricted-role PostgreSQL regression executes both actual query builders for two owners; source-field allowlists alone cannot protect a private title or summary.

## Worker configuration

The worker is disabled when `MINION_PULSE_WORKER_CONFIG` is absent, invalid, disabled or expired. The operator record is strict JSON containing `enabled`, `tenantId`, `ownerProfileId`, `channel`, `gatewayId`, `endpoint`, exact `version`, full 40-character `commit`, `bindingContract: "cron-binding-v1"`, `evidence`, `expiresAt`, and `revision`. Do not put tokens in this record. Configuration is trusted server deployment input; the route accepts no scope query parameters.

GET `/api/pulse/tick` requires the exact `Bearer CRON_SECRET`. It selects only the configured organization, owner and `pulse_dispatch` family, with at most 20 candidates within a 50-second total budget. No schedule is installed by this change. Generic jobs retain their existing tick opt-in and legacy callback budget behavior.

Approval and worker preparation require a current active personal organization, owner membership and fresh Pulse capabilities, enabled module, existing active personal agent, profile binding, user gateway link, exact channel assignment and live gateway lease. Credentials come from that exact row; there is no ambient/system/alternate-gateway fallback. The authenticated `hello-ok.server.version` and full `commit` must exactly match the operator record. The same connected socket is retained across admission and the one `cron.add`; reconnect invalidates preparation.

The request uses flat cron fields, explicit `agentId`, canonical origin `sessionKey`, `scope: "session"`, `sessionTarget: "isolated"`, one-shot `at`, `deleteAfterRun: true` and `delivery: {"mode":"none"}`. Its execution namespace is the Gateway's isolated `agent:<id>:cron:<job>` session, distinct from the origin. Remote success records only a validated scheduling ID. Any ambiguous post-admission outcome becomes indeterminate and cannot automatically retry. Proven pre-admission unavailability retries after 5 seconds and 30 seconds, at most three attempts.

A validated remote ID arriving after revocation is still positive evidence for the immutable admitted attempt. A separate database-only write binds that ID to the original dispatch, admitted generation and frozen target. It cannot change dispatch state or the canceled/successor job's generation, cursor or authority. Scheduled settlement still requires current job ownership. Remote IDs are immutable once recorded; retaining one never authorizes another RPC or proves execution.

## Storage and cancellation

The CLI now imports its existing advisory-lock/recheck/DDL/ledger transaction from `scripts/db-migrate-core.ts`. Its default `public.hub_migrations` ledger, lock key `826744`, file order, transaction rollback and existing version-only bookkeeping remain unchanged. The core accepts a validated, quoted fixture ledger schema only at its internal test call site; the CLI exposes no new flag. Actual SQL and the imported core are tested with independent connections, repeated application, ledger deduplication and failed-DDL rollback. This qualifies the migration seam on PostgreSQL; it does not claim a full Supabase bootstrap.

The additive migration is `supabase/migrations/20261023010000_pulse_dispatch_receipts.sql`; QA seed permutations live in `scripts/qa/seed/pulse.ts`. Proposal revisions, scoped dispatch/proposal/job links, admission state checks, immutable snapshots, attempt limits and a 16 KiB payload bound are enforced in PostgreSQL. Dispatch RLS is forced and binds both organization and actor under `app_ledger`. Ordinary deletion is denied; identity deletion retains dispatch identifiers as historical tombstones. The guarded trigger uses its migration owner's narrowly scoped lookup to verify global job binding without granting `app_ledger` access to the global queue.

Cancellation locks jobs before domain rows, cancels queued dispatches and fences job generations in the same transaction. Admitted work retains an indeterminate tombstone; cancellation never authorizes replay. Account deletion, membership removal and worker reconciliation call this service. Privileged retention may clear raw action payload after 30 days, after fencing active jobs; hashes and receipt identifiers remain. Redaction is irreversible and cannot make a dispatch replayable.

`enqueueJobInTransaction`, `runSelectedTick`, `JobExecution.remainingMs()` and explicit `pulseRetry` handler registration are the shared D/E seam. A postponement callback runs only inside current job ownership, performs database work only, restores the bookkeeping role, and atomically advances the domain attempt/due time together with the job generation and due time.

Selected admission, callback remaining time and the outer tick's authority/retention allowance use a monotonic clock. SQL due times, leases and qualification expiry retain wall-clock timestamps. A backward-clock regression proved that the old candidate admitted a second job after the tick budget expired; the corrected candidate admits one. Separate outer-budget tests subtract authority and retention time and prohibit admission after those phases exhaust 50 seconds.

Selected execution also checks its signal/deadline after the awaited post-claim job read and immediately before invoking the handler. An exhausted read cannot invoke or count work. The existing claim remains resumable with its generation/cursor/attempt bookkeeping intact; no uninvoked job is marked done or granted an immediate replay.
